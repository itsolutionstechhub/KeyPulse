How to Fix Double Clicking Mouse Switch Lube

One of the most common mechanical failures in gaming mice is the dreaded "double-clicking" issue, where pressing a button once triggers two rapid clicks. This is caused by wear, dust, or oxidation forming on the metal contacts inside the mechanical microswitch.

While soldering new switches is the ultimate fix, you can often restore functionality without a soldering iron by cleaning and lubricating the switch contacts. Here is the step-by-step repair guide.

1. Understand why it happens

Most mechanical mice use copper-leaf microswitches (like Omron D2FC). Over time, the copper contacts inside oxidize or lose tension, causing the contacts to bounce and register multiple electrical signals. Introducing contact cleaner and electrical lubricant isolates the contacts and prevents bouncing.

2. Disassemble Your Gaming Mouse

  • Peel off the mouse feet (skates) carefully to locate the hidden screws. (Keep them clean so you can re-apply them later).
  • Remove the screws and gently pull the top shell away from the bottom plate. Be careful of thin ribbon cables connecting the side buttons.
  • Locate the main Left and Right mouse microswitches (small rectangular black boxes with little red, blue, or white buttons).

3. Access the Switch Internals

You do not need to unsolder the switch. Instead, locate the small plastic clips holding the microswitch housing cover. Use a sewing needle or a fine hobby knife to gently pry open the cover. **Warning:** Be extremely careful not to lose the tiny loose plastic button piece that sits inside the cover.

4. Clean and Lubricate the Contacts

Once open, you will see a small, curved copper leaf contact.

  • Spray a tiny drop of electrical contact cleaner directly onto the metal contact point. Click it 20-30 times to clear oxidation.
  • Apply a microscopic drop of specialized synthetic contact lubricant (like DeoxIT or specialized switch lube) to the contact.
  • Re-assemble the switch housing cover, secure the mouse shell, and re-apply the skates.
